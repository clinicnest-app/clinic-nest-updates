X-rays and scans (DICOM)

X-rays, CT and MRI scans from an imaging centre, and the exports of OCT machines and fundus cameras, are usually DICOM files – the format of imaging machines. ClinicNest keeps them with the patient under Photos & documents, shows them in a viewer of its own, and opens them in a full DICOM viewer when you need more.

Their names often end in .dcm. On a CD they often have no ending at all, for example IM000123 – that is fine, ClinicNest recognises them by what is inside.

Add them

Section titled “Add them”

Open the visit, or the patient’s page, and go to Photos & documents.

What the patient brings What to press
A CD or pen drive Add folder (CD, pen drive), then choose the drive or its folder. On a computer only.
A download from the imaging centre’s website, usually a ZIP file Add photo, PDF, DICOM or ZIP, then choose the ZIP. On a computer only.
One or a few files Add photo, PDF, DICOM or ZIP, then choose them. On a computer only.
Only a web address or QR code on the receipt Add link – see below.
A film or a picture on WhatsApp Take photo, or add the picture – see Photos and documents.
  • From a CD or a folder ClinicNest takes only the scan files. The CD’s own viewer program stays behind.
  • The files of one scan are put together: a CT of 300 slices is one entry, not 300. While they are added, the screen shows how far it is (Adding 37 of 312 files …).
  • A file that is added twice is kept once. Files of the same scan added later join it.
  • The entry is named after the scan, for example CT, head, 12 Oct 2026 – what was taken, of which part, when.
  • A file can be up to 50 MB; a scan can have any number of files.

Look at a scan

Section titled “Look at a scan”

Click the entry. The viewer opens.

When the viewer opens, no tool is chosen: dragging up and down on the picture goes through the pictures of a scan (a single picture is moved). Press a tool to drag for that instead – the button turns blue; press it again to put the tool down. Hold the mouse over a button to read what it does.

Button What it does
Brightness and contrast (the sun) Drag down for brighter, up for darker; right for more contrast, left for less. The picture changes at once and stays sharp.
Move (the four arrows) Drag the picture.
Measure (the ruler) Drag from one point to the other. The distance is shown in mm when the file says how large a pixel is. For X-rays it is marked ≈: measured at the detector, so a little larger than in the body.
Angle (the triangle) Click three points: one on the first arm, the corner, one on the second arm. The angle at the corner is shown in degrees. A fourth click starts again.
Zoom in / Zoom out (the magnifying glasses) Also Ctrl and the mouse wheel.
Invert (the half-filled circle) Swaps black and white.
Reset (the round arrow) Brings brightness, contrast, zoom and position back to how the picture was opened, and removes the measured line.
CT presets: Brain, Soft tissue, Lung, Bone (at the right) For a CT only: ready-made brightness and contrast settings. A CT holds far more shades of grey than a screen can show; each button sets them for one kind of tissue.
  • Compare two pictures: press Compare at the bottom of the window. The picture stays at the left; choose any other X-ray, scan or photo of the patient at the right – for example the X-ray before and after treatment. Each side has its own tools. Two scans of many pictures go through together (the right one at the same place in its series); untick Go through both scans together to move them separately. One picture goes back.
  • Print or share: press Print / share at the bottom of the window. The picture is taken as it is on the screen – zoom, brightness, the measured line and angle with their values. Add a note and press Print (a page with the clinic’s letterhead, the patient, the date and the note; the print dialog can also save it as PDF), Save picture (a JPEG to attach, e.g. on WhatsApp Web), or on a phone Share. The patient’s name is only on the printed page, not in the picture. Works for photos too.
  • A larger picture: drag the window’s lower right corner to make the window larger or smaller; the picture follows.
  • A scan with many pictures: the slider at the right edge of the picture goes through them slice by slice, top to bottom; so do the mouse wheel and the arrow keys (Page up and Page down: ten at a time; Home and End: the first and the last). The picture’s number is shown at its lower left, for example Picture 21 / 41.
  • A scan with several series (for example the axial slices and the scout image): the buttons above the picture choose one. It opens on the largest.
  • Esc closes the viewer.

The viewer is for looking at a scan with the patient and checking a report. It is not a radiologist’s workstation.

Open in Weasis (DICOM viewer)

Section titled “Open in Weasis (DICOM viewer)”

For everything a radiologist’s program can do – several series side by side, 3D, many measuring tools – press Open in Weasis (DICOM viewer) in the viewer’s window. Weasis is a free program; it has to be installed once on the computer (Admin guide). The button is shown on computers, not on phones.

Download DICOM gives the file itself – for a scan of many files, all of them in one ZIP – for another DICOM viewer or to hand to the patient.

A link to an online report

Section titled “A link to an online report”

Many imaging centres and labs now give a web address or a QR code on the receipt instead of a CD.

  1. Press Add link.
  2. Paste the address – or press Scan QR and hold the receipt in front of the camera.
  3. Give it a title, for example MRI lumbar spine, City Scans.
  4. In Note, write what the website asks for – often the patient ID and a password printed on the receipt.

Clicking the link later opens the imaging centre’s page in a new tab.

Ask the AI assistant

Section titled “Ask the AI assistant”

Where the AI assistant is on, the viewer’s window has Ask AI. It reads the picture you see, not the file: the patient’s name and ID inside the file stay in the clinic. A name printed into the picture itself would be sent. The assistant describes what it sees; reading the scan stays with you or the radiologist.

Limits

Section titled “Limits”
  • A few files cannot be shown (compressed as JPEG-LS or 12-bit JPEG). They are kept in the record, the window says so, and they can be downloaded or opened in Weasis.
  • Adding scan files needs a computer. On a phone or tablet, add the imaging centre’s link, or take a photo of the film. Scans added on a computer can be looked at on a phone.
  • Large scans take a while to add: the files go one by one.

Who can do this

Section titled “Who can do this”

Doctors and assistants.
